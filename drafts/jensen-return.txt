# Jensen Huang Returns as Nvidia CEO Just 11 Days After Resignation

**November 27, 2026**

Jensen Huang is returning as the Chief Executive Officer of Nvidia, just 11 days after stepping down from the company. Nvidia's board of directors announced his reinstatement on November 23, following a series of discussions with Huang and input from senior US government officials.

Huang's resignation on November 12 came amid controversy surrounding reports about his personal conduct and concerns linked to a possible foreign intelligence operation. His departure had created uncertainty around the leadership of one of the world's most valuable technology companies.

Following his resignation, Nvidia appointed Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO while the board began looking for a permanent replacement.

However, the board reversed its earlier decision and unanimously reinstated Huang. In its official announcement, Nvidia said that discussions with Huang had given the board confidence in his ability to lead the company. It also said that input from senior US government officials indicated that his return did not pose a national security risk.

US President Donald Trump also acknowledged his involvement in discussions surrounding Huang's return. According to reports, the administration supported bringing the Nvidia co-founder back to his leadership position.

The announcement had an immediate impact on the stock market. Nvidia shares rose approximately 31.5% on November 23, adding nearly $1 trillion to the company's market value.

Despite his return, questions remain about Nvidia's corporate governance and the circumstances surrounding his resignation. The company has confirmed that an independent board committee will continue its investigation into the matter. Nvidia has also announced additional oversight measures covering national security and export controls.

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has played a central role in transforming it from a graphics chip manufacturer into a global leader in artificial intelligence computing. The company's GPUs are now widely used to train and run AI models, making it a key player in the global AI industry.

His return provides continuity in Nvidia's leadership at a time when demand for AI computing infrastructure remains high. However, the company will also need to address the governance questions raised during this brief leadership crisis.

For now, Huang is back at the helm of Nvidia, bringing an end to one of the most unexpected leadership developments in the technology industry this year.
